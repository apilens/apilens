APILens has no passwords. You prove who you are with something you already control: your inbox, a passkey on your device, or an account with Google or GitHub.

## Sign up

Enter your work email and select **Continue**. APILens emails you a 6-digit code and a sign-in link. You can also sign up with GitHub or Google if your deployment has them turned on.

<Frame caption="Creating an account takes an email address. There is no password.">
  <img src="/images/auth-sign-up.png" alt="Create your APILens account form with a work email field, Continue button, and GitHub and Google buttons" />
</Frame>

After you verify your email, onboarding asks for your organization and first project. If someone invited you, you join their organization instead.

## Email code or magic link

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your email">
    Select **Email code** on the sign-in page and enter your address. APILens sends the same email whether or not an account exists, so the form never reveals who has an account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the code or open the link">
    Type the 6-digit code, paste the whole code into the first box, or open the link in the email. Codes and links expire after 15 minutes and work once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stay signed in (optional)">
    **Keep me signed in** keeps your session for 30 days. Without it, the session ends after 24 hours.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="Enter the 6-digit code from the email, or open its link instead.">
  <img src="/images/auth-verify.png" alt="Check your inbox screen with six code boxes, Open Gmail and Open Outlook buttons and a resend countdown" />
</Frame>

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| *That code isn't right* | Check the code and try again. |
| *Too many incorrect attempts* | After 5 wrong codes, request a new one. |
| *That code has expired* | Codes last 15 minutes. Request a new one. |
| *Resend in 0:30* | You can request another email after the countdown. |

## Passkeys

A passkey is a key stored by your device or password manager (Touch ID, Windows Hello, iCloud Keychain, 1Password, a security key). It cannot be phished, and it counts as two factors, so APILens never asks for a 2FA code after a passkey sign-in.

- **Add one:** Settings → Security → Passkeys → **Add a passkey**, then follow your browser's prompt. Give it a name you will recognize.
- **Use it:** select **Continue with a passkey** on the sign-in page. Your browser may also offer it when you focus the email field.
- **Remove it:** Settings → Security → Passkeys. You can rename or remove any passkey.

## Google and GitHub

Select **Google** or **GitHub** and approve the request. APILens uses only your verified email address:

- If an APILens account has that email, you sign in to it and the provider is linked.
- Otherwise a new account is created.
- If the provider has no verified email for you, sign-in stops with *Your account with this provider has no verified email*. Verify the address with the provider, or sign in with email.

Link or unlink providers in Settings → Connected accounts.

## Two-factor authentication

Turn on two-factor authentication (TOTP) in Settings → Security → Two-factor:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Scan the QR code">
    Use any authenticator app (1Password, Google Authenticator, Authy, Aegis).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm with a code">
    Enter the 6-digit code your app shows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save your backup codes">
    APILens shows 10 backup codes once. Store them somewhere safe. Each works once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

From then on, signing in with email, Google, GitHub or SSO asks for a code from your app, or a backup code. You have 5 minutes and 5 attempts to finish this step. Passkey sign-ins skip it.

### Lost your authenticator?

Use a backup code. If you have none, select **Lost your device?** on the two-factor step. APILens emails you, waits **24 hours**, then lets you finish recovery: two-factor is turned off and every other session is signed out. The email has a link to cancel if you did not ask for it. The delay means that someone who only has access to your inbox cannot take over your account.

## Sessions

Settings → Sessions lists every device signed in to your account, with its browser, IP address and last activity. You can sign out one session or every other session. A new device signing in triggers an email alert.

Signing out takes effect within seconds everywhere, including for any API calls the session was making.
